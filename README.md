# Driver Incentive Optimization: From Marketplace Data to Business Decision

## Can marketplace data tell us who should receive an incentive?

A marketplace analytics case study demonstrating how data can be transformed into:

**Business Question → Analysis → Insight → Narrative → Business Recommendation**

---

## Executive Summary

In a two-sided marketplace, driver supply directly affects customer experience, fulfillment, and marketplace health.

One challenge with incentive programs is that not every driver responds to incentives in the same way.

A blanket incentive program may increase activity, but it can also spend money on drivers who would have participated anyway.

This case study explores a simple business question:

> **Should marketplace incentives be given broadly to all drivers, or targeted toward drivers who are more likely to change their behavior?**

The analysis uses driver-level behavioral data to:

- establish pre-incentive behavior
- segment drivers
- compare incentive strategies
- measure changes in activity
- identify differences in response
- translate the analysis into a business recommendation

The central analytical principle is:

> **Don't optimize for activity alone. Optimize for incremental activity.**

---

# Business Problem

Imagine a marketplace where driver supply is becoming uneven during certain periods.

Operations wants to increase supply using incentives.

There are two obvious approaches:

### Option 1 — Broad Incentives

Give incentives to a large percentage of the driver base.

### Option 2 — Targeted Incentives

Identify drivers who are more likely to respond and concentrate incentives on those drivers.

The second approach could potentially improve incentive efficiency, but only if the underlying behavioral segmentation is meaningful.

Therefore, the analysis asks:

> **Which driver segments respond differently to incentives, and where could targeted incentives create more incremental activity?**
